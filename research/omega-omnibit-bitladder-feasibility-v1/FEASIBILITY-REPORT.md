## Tagging convention (fixed, applied strictly throughout — this revision removes an earlier draft's blended "MEASURED/CANON" tag)

- **MEASURED** = an empirical result actually produced by running something: our two internal negatives, the Shadow-Resolution capacity curve, or the arXiv:2602.10695 hardware experiment (154 qubits, 77 clones, IBM Heron R2, fidelity 0.286±0.009).
- **CANON** = an established, checkable piece of literature, specification, or proof (a theorem, an RFC, a published spec, a proven cryptographic construction) — real and documented, but not itself a new empirical measurement performed by this scouting pass. Third-party specs (Apache ORC/Parquet), proven constructions (WOTS+, CRT threshold schemes), and theorems (entropy invariance, Kronecker factorization) all belong here, not under "MEASURED."
- **UNVERIFIED** = no real prior art found either way, or a citation that could not be independently confirmed to exist or to apply as claimed.

## 0. The two prior measured negatives — explicitly acknowledged, not glossed over

This proposal is an extension of a system that has **already run two real experiments and gotten real negative results.** Any feasibility verdict on the new idea must be read through those results, not around them.

1. **MEASURED NEGATIVE #1 (unified-omega-v1 core result):** encoding the `LANGUAGE_GENESIS` glyph-permuted, bit-repacked stream through a byte-level entropy coder gave **bpc 4.14** — worse than the plain byte mixer's **bpc 2.65**. Lesson: a bijective relabeling/codebook layer is an **identity layer**, not a compression layer; putting it *before* a byte-context entropy coder destroys the byte structure the coder needs.
2. **MEASURED NEGATIVE #2 (band-ladder omnibit):** a full 64→256→1024→4096 cascade with reversible between-band residual coding was built byte-exact and reversible, and still came out **5.7x worse** than flat entropy coding on the same data (38.9MB vs 6.8MB on 24MB). Root cause already identified: SHA-permutation glyph codebooks destroy byte structure at every band, and roughly 5 parallel streams also multiply output volume ~5x.

**Both negatives are the same failure mode wearing different clothes: a bijective/codebook relabeling layer was asked to do compression's job.**

**Note on causal attribution (corrected on review — a skeptic pass caught this conflation):** the entropy-invariance identity discussed in Section 1 explains why a bijective relabeling layer *cannot help* — i.e., it explains the **direction** of Negative #2's result (worse, not better). It does **not** derive or predict the specific **5.7x magnitude**; that magnitude is better and more directly explained by the separate, non-fundamental implementation fact already stated above (~5 parallel streams multiplying volume ~5x). These are two distinct causal claims — one theoretical (direction), one implementation-specific (magnitude) — and this document no longer presents the theorem as if it derived the specific ratio.

## 1. Subclaim: Native bit-depth floor ladder (2/4/8/16/32/64-bit) below/into the glyph floors

**Verdict: SUPPORTS_WITH_CAVEATS as a native-width ADDRESSING layer; CONTRADICTED as a compression mechanism if it reuses the glyph-codebook mechanism.**

Real, checkable, decades-old prior art strongly supports the *general shape* of a bit-width ladder as an engineering pattern:

- **Apache ORC's RLEv2** encoding enumerates a fixed bit-width table `{1,2,4,8,16,24,32,40,48,56,64}` chosen per data stream from the actual value range (`orc.apache.org/specification/ORCv1`, lane A5). **Apache Parquet's** RLE/Bit-Packing Hybrid computes an adaptive width in `[0,64]` per data page (`apache/parquet-format`, lane A5). Lemire's **SIMD-BP128** (`lemire/simdcomp`; Lemire & Boytsov, *Software: Practice and Experience* 45(1), 2015) packs 128-integer blocks at per-block-minimal widths at >8 billion ints/sec. The lineage traces to Goldstein/Ramakrishnan/Shaft's **Frame of Reference** (ICDE 1998) and Zukowski et al.'s **PFOR** (ICDE 2006). **Tag: CANON** — these are real, published, running-in-production specifications and papers (Spark, Trino, Hive, Iceberg, DuckDB all implement them), not a new measurement produced by this scouting pass. (An earlier draft called this "MEASURED, production-grade, billion-row-scale technology"; that blurred established literature with empirical measurement and has been corrected here.)
- **JPEG2000** (Taubman's EBCOT, IEEE TIP 9(7):1158-1170, 2000; `uclouvain/openjpeg`), **JPEG XL** (`libjxl/libjxl` Squeeze transform, native bit-depths to 32-bit), **FLIF** (any-prefix-decodable, exact lossless), and **SPIHT/EZW** bit-plane coding (Said & Pearlman, IEEE TCSVT 6(3):243-250, 1996) are all real, shipped, lossless-refinement embedded bitstreams (lane A1, A4, CANON).
- **Wavelet trees, Adaptive Radix Trees, HAMTs, Linux's `fib_trie`, sparse voxel octrees** (lane A3, CANON) all support exact multi-granularity reconstruction from one base structure.

**The load-bearing caveat, confirmed by three independent lanes (A1, A5, A8):** every one of these real systems achieves its ladder by varying **numeric precision or spatial sampling density of ONE fixed representation** — none of them layer a **bijective glyph/SHA-permutation codebook** on top to gain compression. That is a structurally different move, and it is precisely the move already tested in this system and found to fail (Negative #2). Formal information theory closes the loop:

- Shannon's noiseless source coding theorem (1948) and the **entropy-invariance theorem** `H(g(X)) = H(X)` for any bijective `g` (Cover & Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed., Ch. 2) — an **identity**, not an approximation. This is CANON, and it directly explains the **direction** of both measured negatives (why relabeling could not have helped), as clarified in Section 0 above.
- Barron, Rissanen & Yu's **MDL two-part-code redundancy theorem** (IEEE Trans. Info. Theory 44(6):2743-2760, 1998, CANON): bounds redundancy for parametric two-part codes in terms of model complexity `k` (`≈ (k/2)log(n)`). **Corrected on review:** applying this to "each added relabeling level in our ladder costs overhead" is this report's own **analogical extension** of the theorem, not a literal restatement of it — an earlier draft's phrasing ("PROVES...a redundancy TAX proportional to the number of levels added") overstated how directly the theorem applies and has been softened here. The qualitative conclusion (added parametric/model layers add overhead absent captured structure) is a reasonable analogy to draw from the theorem; it is not something the theorem itself proves about this specific architecture.
- Cover's **enumerative source encoding** (1973, CANON): permutation-indexing schemes reach the entropy bound exactly — they never beat it.

**Conclusion:** the lower rungs of the proposed ladder are architecturally sound **only** if implemented as native-integer-width block selection (Parquet/ORC/PFOR-style — pick the minimal width from the block's actual data range, no relabeling). If the ladder is instead conceived as "more of the same" bijective SHA-permutation-codebook mechanism already used for the 256/1024/4096 glyph floors, continued downward through 2/4/8/16/32/64-bit, it will very likely reproduce Negative #2's **direction** (worse, not better) — the specific magnitude of any new failure is a separate, unpredicted quantity that would require its own measurement.

## 2. Subclaim: Q-Prism 3D-slice bridging mechanism ("sits between levels")

**Verdict: SUPPORTS_WITH_CAVEATS.**

The existing Shadow-Resolution / Q-Prism CRT-prime-cylinder family already rests on real, well-established mathematics:

- **Mignotte (1982)** and **Asmuth-Bloom (1983)** CRT-based threshold secret sharing (lane B1, B3, B8, CANON) formally prove the reconstructible range grows with the **product of combined coprime moduli** — an additive-in-log2-per-modulus law that qualitatively **matches the shape** of the system's own already-measured ~50-bit-at-2-cylinders / ~75-bit-at-3-cylinders curve. (This grounds the *shape* of the curve in real math; it does not externally replicate the *specific numbers*, which remain an internal MEASURED result of this system, not corroborated elsewhere.)
- **Ramp secret sharing** (Blakley & Meadows, CRYPTO '84, CANON) formalizes graded partial-recovery as an established scheme class, with real implementations (`junkurihara/PySSS`).
- **Reed-Solomon / erasure coding** (`klauspost/reedsolomon`, Intel ISA-L, `tahoe-lafs/zfec`; McEliece & Sarwate, CACM 24(9):583-584, 1981, proving Shamir secret sharing *is* an MDS/Reed-Solomon code) and **rateless fountain codes** (Luby's LT codes, FOCS 2002; Shokrollahi's Raptor codes, IEEE Trans. Info. Theory 52(6), 2006; RFC 6330 RaptorQ) independently confirm flexible multi-shadow recovery is mature, production-grade engineering (lane B2, B6, CANON).
- **Zarr v3 sharding (ZEP0002)**, Google's **TensorStore** `chunk_layout`, and **Neuroglancer's** octree LOD format (lane E1, CANON) give real, currently-running precedent for nesting one addressing granularity inside another under one shared root as a lossless index — the closest concrete analogue to "Q-Prism sits between levels."

**Scope note (made explicit in this revision to align the body text with this report's own Section 8 tag, which a skeptic pass correctly noted the body text did not yet clearly reflect):** none of the systems above actually bridges a **heterogeneous native-bit-width ladder** (2/4/8/16/32/64-bit through the 256/1024/4096 glyph floors) the way this proposal envisions. That specific composition is **untested synthesis — UNVERIFIED** — not something any cited source has built or measured. The support above is for the general mechanism *class* (CRT/ramp/erasure-code multi-shadow recovery; nested addressing under one shared root), not for this specific bridge design.

**Caveat, consistent with (not contradicting) the system's own negatives:** every one of these is a **redundancy/addressing/recovery** mechanism, never a compression mechanism. Where real pyramid/mipmap systems (lane A6) achieve *exact* reversibility across levels (Laplacian pyramids, JPEG2000's reversible 5/3 wavelet, Hoppe's Progressive Meshes), they buy it only via **explicit residual retention at a measured cost** — never for free. Fractal/self-similarity compression (lane A7) only yields real gain when it discovers **actual data-dependent redundancy**. **Q-Prism bridging is well-grounded as an addressing/recovery layer in general; nothing found licenses expecting it to also compress, and the specific ladder-bridging composition proposed here remains unverified.**

## 3. Subclaim: Omega family unification (CUBE_8POLE + TRI_12SECTOR + PI_20LENS, "quanted omega omnibit")

**Verdict: SUPPORTS_WITH_CAVEATS.**

The *already-sealed* part of this — combining independently-computed opaque commitment roots into one shared root via `omega = H(D || e || root || sorted[omega8, omega12, omegaPi])` — is strongly grounded:

- **Merkle-tree/Merkle-DAG commitment** (Merkle, CRYPTO '89 / 1979 manuscript; the git object model; IPFS, Benet, arXiv:1407.3561; RFC 6962/9162 Certificate Transparency) is real, running at internet scale, and establishes "commitment/seed, never reversible encoding" as standard practice, not aspiration (lane C5, CANON).
- **Merkle Mountain Range peak-bagging** (`nervosnetwork/merkle-mountain-range`, live in Grin/Mimblewimble) and **Ethereum's** `stateRoot`/`transactionsRoot`/`receiptsRoot` composition are near-literal precedent for hashing several independently-produced sub-roots together into one root (lane C6, E5, CANON).
- **escnn** (`QUVA-Lab/escnn`, ICLR 2022, CANON) hosts both octahedral and icosahedral/continuous symmetry groups inside one steerable-CNN framework, measured on real benchmarks per-group.
- The 2026 **Group-Algebraic Tensors** preprint (arXiv:2605.20440 — independently re-verified: submitted 19 May 2026, authors from UT Austin/IBM Research/Tufts University/Tel Aviv University; **peer review status NOT established**, the same caveat class this report already applied to the quantum-cloning paper family, extended here on review) formally proves, as a theorem, a **Kronecker factorization** composing multiple finite groups' tensor algebras into one framework (lane C1, CANON). This is real support for composability **in principle**. **Corrected on review:** the paper's own empirical demonstration (QM9 molecular geometry, recovering Wigner-Eckart selection rules) is run over a **single group** — the chiral octahedral subgroup of SO(3) — not an actual measured fusion of two heterogeneous polyhedral families analogous to CUBE_8POLE+TRI_12SECTOR. An earlier draft's phrase "an actively-published, measured pattern" overstated this paper's empirical scope; the accurate characterization is: theoretically composable per a proven theorem, empirically demonstrated for one group only.

**Two important caveats:**

1. **Geometric correction worth checking (lane C3):** the operator's own description of TRI_12SECTOR is "12 edges of a six-square-pyramid cube decomposition" — i.e., cube-derived. Standard polytope theory says a cube's 8 vertices, 12 edges, and 6 faces are already **three orbits of the SAME group**, the full octahedral group `Oh` (order 48) — concretely realized as the cuboctahedron (12 vertices) and rhombic dodecahedron (12 faces), both textbook `Oh`-symmetric, **not** icosahedral. If this is literally what TRI_12SECTOR is, **no bridge to a different exceptional finite group is mathematically necessary at all** — this would be a *simplification* opportunity, not evidence the bridge is unsound. If a genuinely separate icosahedral/dodecahedral family is intended instead, note that octahedral and icosahedral groups are **not nested in one another** (classification of finite subgroups of SO(3)); the only established formal bridge is the much weaker McKay/ADE correspondence (`Oh` ↔ affine `Ẽ7`, `I/Ih` ↔ affine `Ẽ8`), which keeps each group's identity fully separate rather than merging them.
2. **Naive shared-root fusion has a real, category-matched operational counter-lesson.** At the level of real, adversarially-hardened production systems, the most battle-tested large-scale answer to "many heterogeneous families, one addressing scheme" is actually **"one shared verification protocol, separate roots per family"** (Cosmos IBC's `ics23`, Google Trillian's per-type "personalities") rather than literal merger (lane E5, CANON) — a real operational counter-lesson worth weighing against the ambition to physically fuse `omega8`/`omega12`/`omegaPi` into one interior commitment rather than composing them at an outer layer (which is, notably, what the *already-sealed* unified-Omega-core formula already does correctly).

**Correction (removed on review):** an earlier draft of this section also cited PCGrad-style gradient-conflict literature (Yu et al., NeurIPS 2020) as a caution against fusing heterogeneous families into one root. On review, that citation does not mechanistically apply here: PCGrad's finding concerns conflicting gradients across **shared trainable parameters** during neural-network optimization; a deterministic hash commitment `H(D||e||root||sorted[...])` has no gradients, no training dynamics, and no shared trainable parameters, so PCGrad's mechanism cannot transfer to it. It has been dropped as a category-mismatched analogy rather than retained as supporting evidence — the real, category-matched caution is the IBC/Trillian operational point above, which is kept.

All group-symmetry combination mechanisms found keep every component SIMULTANEOUSLY accessible; none support single-use/selective gating (see the quantum-key-bridge subclaim below).

## 4. Subclaim: Quantum-encrypted-cloning "single-use key" analogue for bridging families

**Verdict: SUPPORTS_WITH_CAVEATS as metaphor; bounded by a real impossibility result if taken as a literal static mechanism.**

arXiv:2602.10695 is confirmed real. **Corrected on review:** independent re-check finds it cited by **two** genuinely independent, differently-authored follow-on preprints — arXiv:2604.10155 and arXiv:2606.06552 (the authors of these follow-ons, e.g. Gianini/Cimato/Hasan/Damiani/Lin/Mio, are a genuinely different group from the original paper's authors, so this is real independent engagement, not self-citation). A third identifier used in an earlier draft, **arXiv:2605.27421, does not resolve to any findable paper** on repeated re-check (including a direct search for that exact ID) and has been **dropped rather than retained as a soft citation**, per this report's own claims-gate rule to mark unconfirmed identifiers `verifiable=false` and exclude them. Both confirmed follow-ons are themselves still-unreviewed preprints — real, checkable documents (CANON), peer review status NOT established, same as the base paper — and are **theoretical/analytical follow-on work, not new hardware measurements**; Section 8 now tags them accordingly rather than blending them into "MEASURED."

The base paper's 154-qubit/77-clone IBM Heron R2 claim is consistent with real, documented hardware specs (156-qubit heavy-hex device, ~0.3% two-qubit gate error — lane D3). **Corrected on review:** this is stated here as a **qualitative consistency check only** — the gate-error spec and the 0.286±0.009 fidelity figure are not in tension — rather than as the gate error "explaining" the fidelity; no circuit-depth/error-budget calculation deriving that specific number from that specific error rate was performed or found, so "consistent with" is the accurate verb, not "explaining." Its "maximally-mixed unselected marginals, no no-cloning violation" framing is independently engaged by follow-on work (arXiv:2604.10155 ties it toward AME states / quantum secret sharing) while also documenting a real caveat — not every unauthorized subset is fully non-informative — so the HARD BOUNDARY as stated in this task is honest and correctly scoped, not a stretch (lane D4).

**The operator's own framing — "analogous in SPIRIT, not claimed as physically real" — is the correct posture, and real classical cryptography gives it solid footing, with one hard boundary:**

- Real classical analogues for "reveal exactly one of N, the rest locked out" exist and are decades-old: **1-out-of-N Oblivious Transfer** (`sdiehl/oblivious-transfer`, `osu-crypto/libOTe`), **All-or-Nothing Disclosure of Secrets** (Brassard, Crépeau, Robert, CRYPTO '86), and **One-Time Programs / One-Time Memory** (Goldwasser, Kalai, Rothblum, CRYPTO 2008) (lane D2, CANON).
- **Correction on review — attribution fix:** GKR 2008 defines the OTP concept and the one-time-memory (OTM) hardware-token primitive as a **theoretical black box** (each OTM stores two secret strings, reveals exactly one on a single-bit request, then self-destructs). The 2008 paper itself is a theoretical construction, **not** a hardware demonstration. An earlier draft of this report incorrectly stated GKR 2008 "includes a real hardware implementation"; independent re-check found the actual physical hardware implementation/evaluation work is separate, later literature (e.g., a 2010 paper on garbled circuits for leakage-resilience covering hardware implementation and evaluation of one-time programs), which this correction now attributes accurately rather than to GKR 2008 itself.
- **Broadbent, Gutoski & Stebila (CRYPTO 2013 — lane D2)**, independently re-verified against the paper's own published abstract, establish **two linked findings**, and the authors' own abstract explicitly labels the **second** as "the main result" — not the first: (1) a real, citable impossibility point: one-time programs for classical or quantum circuits based *solely on quantum information*, with no hardware device, do not exist, even granting computational assumptions — correcting a naive intuition that quantum no-cloning alone might suffice; and (2) the paper's own stated main result, a **positive construction**: any quantum circuit can be compiled into a one-time program using the same basic one-time-memory (OTM) hardware device already used for classical circuits. **Correction on review:** an earlier draft of this report inverted that emphasis, presenting finding (1) as though it were the paper's central proven contribution. Finding (1) is real and remains the operative hard boundary for a trust-anchor-free static design — but the paper itself is, in its own framing, more optimistic about one-time programs than that inverted emphasis suggested, provided a hardware trust anchor is accepted. This remains a real, hard, citable boundary directly relevant if "quanted omega omnibit" is imagined as a static deterministic hash/keyed transform with no external trust anchor and no interactive protocol: that specific target is proven-impossible under finding (1), not merely unbuilt.
- Compartmented/hierarchical classical secret sharing (Simmons 1988, Tassa 2004/2007, FROST — lane B7; HKDF, BIP-32, ABE — lane D8, all CANON) gives real, well-established precedent for the OTHER half of the analogy (distinct local key per family, shared reconstructable root) but explicitly **without** any consumption/single-use property — classical shares/keys are never "used up."
- The single strongest real classical primitive for genuine one-time-then-broken semantics is **CANON**, decades-proven, and needs no quantum hardware at all: **WOTS+** (Winternitz one-time signatures, foundational to XMSS/LMS/SLH-DSA, RFC 8391/8554) — provably insecure to reuse by construction (lane D7). If the design genuinely wants "one authorized use, then locked," this is the correct, already-proven classical primitive to build on.

**Net:** the analogy is defensible as inspiration/metaphor for graded, threshold-style access structures (real classical math is available and well-trodden for that). It should not be read as license to build a literal single-use static hash-transform gate — that specific target has a real, formal impossibility result standing against it (Broadbent-Gutoski-Stebila's finding (1) specifically, a real secondary result of that paper, not its headline result) unless an interactive protocol or external trust anchor is added.

## 6. Does the new idea avoid repeating the two measured negatives?

**Only conditionally — and the condition is not yet guaranteed by the proposal as worded.**

- **Negative #2 (band-ladder, 5.7x worse)** was a full reversible cascade using SHA-permutation glyph codebooks *as the mechanism* at every band. The new proposal's lower rungs (2/4/8/16/32/64-bit) avoid repeating this **if and only if** they are implemented as native-integer-width block-packing in the Parquet/ORC/PFOR sense (addressing only, zero relabeling) — real prior art (lane A5) shows this is a sound, different mechanism from the one that already failed. The proposal's own wording — "continuing up through the existing glyph floors 256, 1024, 4096" — risks treating the native-width ladder and the glyph-codebook ladder as **one continuous mechanism**. Per lane A8's information theory, that would not be a new risk, it would be the *same* proven failure-direction extended one rung further (its magnitude would still need its own measurement, per Section 0's correction).
- **Negative #1 (unified-omega-v1, bpc 4.14)** was a bijective relabeling layer placed *before* a byte-level entropy coder, destroying the byte structure the coder needed. The Q-Prism bridging and quantized-omega-omnibit fusion function avoid repeating this **as long as they are kept strictly as addressing/commitment/recovery mechanisms** (per the support in lane C5/C6/B1-B8) and never fed through, or expected to feed, a downstream entropy coder as a compression step. Nothing in the proposal as described asks for this — but nothing in it explicitly forbids it either, and the risk is real enough (both negatives were "someone tried to get compression out of an addressing layer") that it should be an explicit, written design invariant, not an implicit assumption.

**Design discipline required to actually avoid both negatives, stated plainly:** the native bit-depth floors, the Q-Prism bridge, and the quantized omega-omnibit must all be built and reasoned about as an **addressing/commitment/identity organ** (real, well-supported prior art throughout lanes A5, B1-B8, C5, C6, E1) — never as a **compression organ** (the job real prior art consistently assigns to a separate, flat, byte-context entropy coder, e.g. the existing mix/SGRAM). This is exactly the distinction the system already learned the hard way twice; the new idea does not violate it by design, but it also does not yet enforce it by design.

## 7. Overall feasibility

**POSSIBLE_WITH_CAVEATS.** Large parts of this proposal reuse real, decades-proven, independently verifiable architecture (native bit-width block packing; CRT/ramp/erasure-code shadow recovery; Merkle-root-of-roots commitment combination; compartmented/hierarchical classical secret sharing for distinct-key-per-family). The genuinely novel pieces — a bit-depth ladder that continues into the glyph floors as one mechanism, a bespoke "quanted omega omnibit" transition function, and single-use asymmetric access with no trust anchor — are either unverified novel synthesis (no prior art found either way) or specifically bounded/contradicted by real theory (information-theoretic entropy invariance for the ladder-as-compression reading; the Broadbent-Gutoski-Stebila impossibility finding for a literal static single-use gate). None of this is a reason to abandon the direction; it is a reason to gate the next build step narrowly (see the proposed minimal experiment) before investing in the full ladder + bridge + fusion stack.

## 10. Recommended operational structure — LIRIS seat framing (contributed same session, 2026-07-17)

Independently of this scout survey, the LIRIS seat reviewed the operator's proposal and converged on the **same discipline from a different angle** — worth stating explicitly because two independent methods (a 40-lane external prior-art survey here; a first-principles structural read there) landing on the same requirement is a real corroboration signal, not a coincidence to bury.

LIRIS's framing: treat the proposal as a **typed lattice of coupled manifolds, not one merged space**:

- **Floor axis:** 64 → 256 → 1024 → 4096 (and, per this report's Section 1, only soundly extendable downward as native-width block packing, not glyph-codebook relabeling).
- **Family axis:** 8-pole / 12-sector / 20-lens / path axis (explicitly tagged, not silently conflated — see this report's Section 3 geometric caveat on whether 12-sector is actually a second orbit of the *same* octahedral group as 8-pole, or a genuinely distinct family).

Not asserted from a diagram sketch — proven, with the bounds stated.

**Where this converges with the scout findings above:** both arrive at "prove it before you declare it," from opposite directions —

- LIRIS's transfer-axis requirement (an explicit, deterministic, provably-commuting map) is the formal-structure version of what Section 4 already found empirically bounded: a literal static, non-interactive, no-trust-anchor "single-use gate" between families is **proven impossible** (Broadbent-Gutoski-Stebila, CRYPTO 2013, finding (1)).
